The Bending Subsidy Curve

Why OSF's own investment in research shrinks — as a share of the whole — even as the program grows.

What this shows — in plain terms

Research is paid for two ways. Most comes from outside — grants, government, and industry sponsors. OSF adds its own money, the ministry subsidy, to cover the parts that outside funding does not (start-up costs, systems, and staff time not yet billed to a study). Each bar below is one year; the two colors add up to the total spent on research that year:

Outside funding — grants + industry (grows over time) OSF subsidy — the money OSF puts in Subsidy share — OSF's slice, right-hand scale

The point: OSF's subsidy is a temporary bridge, not a forever cost. As outside funding grows and we get better at billing study costs back to sponsors, OSF's share (the gold line) bends downward.
